Many Shopify teams translate blog copy first and think about URLs later. That order usually creates cleanup work. If the article is localized but the handle stays in the source language, search intent, click-through trust, and internal consistency all become weaker.

<Callout title="Short version">
  <p>Decide your market URL structure first. Then decide which blog handles should stay stable, which should be localized, and which old URLs need redirects before you publish.</p>
</Callout>

## Start with structure, not article wording

Before touching any blog handle, decide how each market will be exposed. In practice, that usually means choosing whether the localized blog will live in market subdirectories, market domains, or the URL pattern already created by Shopify Markets.

If that part is unstable, article-level SEO work becomes fragile too. A clean localized handle helps only when the full route structure also tells search engines which market the page belongs to.

## What should actually be translated

For blog SEO, there are usually three layers:

1. The article title shoppers read.
2. The meta title and description search engines read.
3. The URL handle that becomes part of the permanent path.

The first two should almost always be localized. The third should be localized only when the target market truly searches in its own language and the new slug improves clarity.

## When translating the handle is worth it

Translate the blog article handle when all three conditions are true:

- the target audience searches with local-language keywords
- the current source-language slug has little or no accumulated authority in that market
- the translated version can stay short, readable, and keyword-relevant

Examples:

- Good: `/pt-br/blogs/news/guia-de-tamanho-infantil`
- Weak: `/pt-br/blogs/news/complete-definitive-childrens-sizing-guide-for-seasonal-shopping`

The goal is not literal translation. The goal is a clean local query match.

## When keeping the source handle is safer

Sometimes the better move is to keep the original handle and localize everything else around it.

That is usually safer when:

- the article already has backlinks and rankings
- the topic is brand-led rather than search-led
- the translated phrase would become long or unnatural
- multiple teams would struggle to maintain redirect discipline

In those cases, a strong localized title, description, body copy, internal links, and hreflang setup can still do most of the SEO work.

## The rule for Shopify handles

Treat Shopify handles as editorial assets, not as raw translations.

That means:

- keep them lowercase
- keep them short
- use hyphens consistently
- remove filler words
- prefer search terms people actually use in that market
- avoid translating brand names, SKU logic, or internal identifiers

If you would not want to place the slug in a navigation menu, it is probably too awkward for the URL as well.

## Blog-specific SEO details teams often miss

On Shopify blogs, the visible article copy is only one part of the page. Teams often forget to localize the supporting SEO layer too:

- article title
- meta title
- meta description
- article handle
- internal links pointing to related posts
- canonical mapping
- hreflang mapping
- sitemap discoverability

If one of those layers stays in the source market, the page can feel half-localized even when the body content reads well.

## A practical rollout strategy

The cleanest rollout is usually:

1. Export the blog articles you plan to localize.
2. Mark which posts are search-driven and which are brand-driven.
3. Translate titles, meta fields, and handles for the search-driven group first.
4. Prepare redirect mappings from old URLs to new localized equivalents.
5. Publish hreflang and canonical relationships at the same time.
6. Update sitemap coverage and internal links after publishing.

That sequence reduces the chance of publishing a new localized slug that search engines cannot understand or visitors cannot reach.

## The biggest mistakes

### Literal handle translation

Literal slugs are often too long. They also miss how people really search. Use the market's keyword phrasing, not sentence-level translation.

### Redirecting late

If you change an indexed handle without mapping the old URL, you turn existing authority into avoidable leakage.

### Mixing conventions

Some teams localize a few slugs, leave some in English, and use underscores or inconsistent casing elsewhere. Search engines can still crawl that, but it looks messy and becomes hard to govern.

### Forgetting the blog archive layer

The article slug is not the only concern. Blog category paths, archive links, and related-post links also need to stay coherent after localization.

## A better governance rule

You do not need to localize every blog handle. You need a repeatable policy.

A practical policy looks like this:

- translate search-led articles for markets with strong local-language demand
- keep existing high-authority handles stable unless there is a clear upside
- apply one slug convention per market
- never change indexed URLs without redirect mapping

That is usually enough to keep multilingual SEO clean without creating unnecessary migration work.
